3. **First-order test, fixed before the data.** (a) `--probe 1` at the base (OUTDIR/probe.jsonl) prints |F_pred| for a = 1/64, 1/32, 1/16 in double-double and quad-double; (b) the chain's own attempts record |F_pred| at each tried a (1.0, then halvings). 4. **The chain**: three steps (D 78, 79, 80 if every step is accepted at a = 1). A step is **converged** when its qd max|F| <= 1e-44 (`--tol_qd`); a failed attempt is halved up to three times (anm_run's rule), and the history of every attempt (a, |F_pred|, |F| per Newton step, outcome) is reported. "Gets past D 78" means a converged state at a D > 78.0 or at 78.0.
5. **Control**: the base polish (`--base_polish 1`, the default) re-converges the D 77 state in the full system before the first step; |Delta_base - Delta_state| must be <= 1e-24 (the plan floor; #641's control was 7e-29), else it is reported.
6. **Where a step converges at an integer D**: Delta_full - Delta_held, with Delta_held from `T138-win2/chain/anm_pin3_D7X.0000.npz` (and `anm_pin3_D80.0000.npz`), compared with #641's `--kfreeze 7` shift (the partial release of harmonics 5 and 7): **+3.665e-14 at D 80** (D 100: +9.694e-13), and with #642's full-release shifts at D 74 to 77 (+4.62e-16, +1.252e-15, +2.432e-15, +4.062e-15).
7. **A run that has not finished after 4 h** (the estimate is 2 h) is stopped and reported with what it printed.

## Amendment 3, 2026-11-13 17:15 UTC, after the whole ladder had been read

Amendment 2's rule said "first order if s <= 1.3", with no lower bound on s, so a flat series would be called first order. The ladder is flat (slope -0.11, below), which the letter of the rule would call "first order". That is a defect of my rule, not a result: a first-order error is |F| proportional to a (s about 1). I read "flat" as its own class, s < 0.7, and `table_682.py` (with its selftest, which now includes a flat series that must come out FLAT) prints both readings. The class "FLAT" says the quad-double |F| of the prediction shows no a-dependence at all above the floor's noise, which excludes a first-order and a second-order error at the resolution of the floor.
