**The conjugate symmetry, read off the caches** (a side check; the strip no longer rests on it, since every lower circle was run). The nine circles repeat their upper twins' evaluation counts (22 to 29, equal circle by circle) and max steps (equal to 1e-3 rad). A first check, `sym_582.py`, compared the cached value at each lower point with the upper twin's at the matched conjugate point, with the bound written beforehand, |d ln|det|| and |arg sum| < 1e-6. **It fails** (`sym_582_result.txt`): of 330 distinct matched points, 253 agree and 77 differ, by up to 2.7e-3 in ln|det| and 3.4e-3 in arg (over #582's nine circles and #574's three mirrors; this check already fails on the real data, so its own control `--mutate=arg` cannot discriminate, and the three steps below carry the controls). The cause, established in three steps:

1. The differing points are exactly those whose two arguments are not bit-for-bit conjugates (an ulp apart in Re or Im, 4.4e-16 in Re at the point of step 3; probably the rounding of the mirrored circle's `cos` and `sin`, which I did not check): `ulp_split_582.py` finds 253 bit-identical conjugate points all symmetric, 77 not bit-identical all asymmetric, 0 and 0 in the other cells (its control `--mutate=bump` fails with 5 asymmetric bit-identical points).
2. At the exact conjugate argument the symmetry holds: `exactconj_582.py` evaluates det at conj(lam) for the six points of largest cached asymmetry, and ln|det| agrees to the last bit (difference 0) and |arg sum| is 6.5e-11 to 7.9e-11 (`exactconj_582_result.txt`); its control `--mutate=ulp` (Re of the conjugate shifted by one ulp) fails at 2.5e-4 to 1.2e-3 in all six (`exactconj_582_ulp_result.txt`).
3. `conj_diag_582.py` at one point (2.7387538820250192, -0.9076196541337447): re-evaluation reproduces ln|det| 0.87570393258164 to the bit; the upper point cached by #574, 4.4e-16 away in Re, gives 0.8766878755904877 (9.8e-4 apart); shifting Re by 1e-13 gives 0.87570005 (3.9e-6 apart) and by 1e-9 gives 0.87599744 (2.9e-4 apart) (`conj_diag_582_result.txt`).

So det M is exactly conjugate-symmetric (ln|det| bit for bit, arg to 8e-11), and the evaluation is **noise-limited at about 1e-3 in ln|det| and in arg**: a change of the argument by one ulp changes the value by up to 3e-3. The windings do not depend on it (max step 0.39 rad, the tolerance pi/4 = 0.785). What it limits is how well a zero position is known from a circle, and the quoted estimates (n0/2 against n0 sums) are the circle's own measure of that. I have not found where the noise comes from: it is consistent with rounding amplified by the matrix's conditioning (the pivot ratio pmax/pmin is about 1e20 to 1e22 on these circles, double-double arithmetic), and I did not test that. The 7e-11 offset in |arg sum| at the exact conjugate is also unexplained.
